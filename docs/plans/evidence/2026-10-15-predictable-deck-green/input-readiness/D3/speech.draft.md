## Slide 1: One account across providers

Hi everyone.

Let me show you the access journey we are proposing. Imagine a colleague who needs to open two different providers. This is a simple illustration.

The idea is that the colleague uses their own account for both. Let us follow that journey one step at a time.

## Slide 2: Start with your account

First, our colleague starts with their own account.

Signing in lets the service recognise who is arriving. In this proposed journey, that is the account the colleague will use as they move between providers.

Now they can choose the first provider.

## Slide 3: Open a provider

Now the colleague chooses the provider they need.

The proposal keeps a separate access check there. Having an account answers who the person is; the provider still needs to decide whether the person may use it.

That matters when the colleague moves to another provider.

## Slide 4: Switch to another provider

So, the colleague moves to the second provider using the same account.

That does not mean the two providers give the colleague the same access. Each provider can have its own permissions.

We also have not settled every sign-in detail. Using the same account alone does not tell us whether another sign-in step will appear. What stays clear is the personal account and the separate provider access.

## Slide 5: What this changes

That is the access journey in the proposal: the person carries one account across providers, and provider access remains a separate question.

For operations, the support path will matter too. We still need to work out how account questions and provider access questions reach the right help.

Is the journey clear, and where would someone need more guidance?
